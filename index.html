Research

Segregated Workplaces and the Careers of Less-Educated Workers

with Edward L. Glaeser and William R. Kerr

Work in Progress

Does workplace segregation impact workers' careers? We use the Longitudinal Employer-Household Dynamics database to measure the high and rising levels of workplace isolation of non-college workers. We find a strong positive relationship between exposure to educated co-workers at the establishment level and future career trajectories of workers without a college degree.

Paper Code

Venture Capital, Local Job Growth, and Blitz Scaling Firms

with Edward L. Glaeser, William R. Kerr, and Lydia Qin

Work in Progress

Using a novel dataset matching venture capital investments to Census Bureau datasets, we quantify the changing impact of venture capital investments on local job growth within cities and its relationship to rising real estate prices.

Positioned at Extremes: Future Job Placements of Immigrant Students at US Colleges

with Sari Pekkala Kerr, William R. Kerr, and Andrew Wang

Internal Migration Review, November 2025

Immigrant students who attend US colleges are disproportionately employed in either large firms, especially multinationals, or small firms and self-employment. Using linked Census and longitudinal employment data, we trace the jobs taken by college students in 2000 during the 2001–2020 period.

Paper

Workplace Segregation between College and Noncollege Workers

with Edward L. Glaeser and William R. Kerr

AEA Papers and Proceedings, Vol. 115, pp. 139–145, May 2025

We measure the level and growth of educational segregation in US workplaces from 2000 to 2020. Workplace isolation was particularly high for young and male workers without college degrees, and the isolation of noncollege workers is increasing over time.
